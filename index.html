MBID ideation system

MBID stands for Multifunctional Bio-inspired Design - a biological morphology-informed, rule-based concept design framework developed to translate functional biological structural strategies into novel morphology-driven multifunctional bio-inspired device architectures.

APPLICATION

MBID ideation system operates at the early stages of innovation, supporting concept definition, embodiment development, and functional verification. Concepts demonstrating technical feasibility and strategic potential advance to detailed product development and commercialisation

MBID applies to industrial sectors where multifunctionality is achieved through morphology-driven design, including aerospace, defence, medical and surgical devices, automotive and marine systems, wearable technologies, bio-inspired robotics, energy systems, and resource-constrained environments.

FOUNDATION

The MBID ideation system guides the generation of morphology-driven multifunctional bio-inspired device concepts by systematically integrating biological morphological strategies into engineered geometry–material configurations to achieve coupled functional performance.

The effectiveness of the MBID ideation system is evidenced through cross-sector application and functional validation, including medical and surgical devices (painless meso-scale sutures), passive robotic devices (non-pneumatic tyres), aerospace thermal-management devices (heat-absorbing, low-pressure-drop architectures), and multifunctional wearable devices (marine-adaptive skins and impact-protection helmets).

DESIGN METHODOLOGY

The core of the system is a methodical framework comprising Domain Integrated Design (DID) Method and Expandable Domain Integrated Design (xDID) Model, which guide users in the systematic exploration and integration of functional biological morphologies.

At the core of the DID and xDID methodologies is the classification of biological morphological features based on their defining characteristics and their mapping to overarching geometric/architectural designations, referred to as Domains. These Domains are defined as Surfaces, Cellular Structures, Shapes, and Cross-Sections.

BIKAS - MORPHOLOGICAL DOMAIN INTEGRATION KNOWLEDGE BASE

BIKAS stands for Biologically Inspired Knowledge Acquisition and Simulacrum is the morphological domain integration knowledge base of the MBID framework. It comprises biological morphological features that are abstracted, classified, and mapped to their respective geometric/architectural designations.

Morphological features are organised under four geometric designations: Surfaces Domain, Cellular Structures Domain, Shapes Domain, and Cross-sections Domain.

BIKAS currently includes 57 morphological features. The feature set expands, and the domain mappings are refined as the methodology evolves.

The web application presents BIKAS through tree dendrogram visualisations. These dendrograms represent the hierarchical organisation of morphological features, structured using a logic analogous to agglomerative clustering. Users navigate these designations to explore, analyse, and integrate morphology-informed design strategies.
