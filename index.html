MBID stands for Multifunctional Bio-inspired Design - a biological morphology-informed, rule-based concept design framework developed to translate functional biological structural strategies into novel multifunctional mechanical architectures. The MBID applies to any field that benefits from multifunctional, morphology-driven structures, including aerospace & defense, medical & surgical devices, bio-inspired robotics (soft & micro), and energy & environmental systems.

CORE THEME

The core theme of the MBID ideation system is the generation of multifunctional bio-inspired structures through the structured combination of biological (morphological) features.

The effectiveness of the MBID ideation system is demonstrated through its application to the design and functional validation of concepts across multiple sectors, including medical & surgical instruments such as painless meso-scale sutures, passive multifunctional structures for robotics such as non-pneumatic tyres, aerospace components such as heat-absorbing structures, wearable structures such as marine skins and protective helmets.

DESIGN METHODOLOGY

The MBID ideation system provides a structured approach that bridges biological morphology with design and engineering, from early-stage problem definition to embodiment design with functional verification. The core of the system is a methodical framework comprising Domain Integrated Design (DID) and Expandable Domain Integrated Design (xDID), which guide users in the systematic exploration and integration of functional morphologies.

At the core of the DID / xDID methodologies is the classification of morphological features based on their defining characteristics and their mapping to overarching architectural / geometric designations, referred to as Domains. These Domains are defined as Surfaces, Cellular Structures, Shapes, and Cross-Sections.

BIKAS KNOWLEDGE BASE

BIKAS stands for Biologically Inspired Knowledge Acquisition and Simulacrum is the core knowledge base of the MBID framework. It is an evolving repository of biological morphological features that are abstracted, classified, and mapped based on their overarching architectural / geometric designations.

The web application presents the BIKAS knowledge base using tree dendrogram visualisations. These dendrograms depict the hierarchical organisation of morphological features, constructed using an approach analogous to agglomerative clustering.
