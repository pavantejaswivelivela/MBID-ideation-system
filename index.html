MBID ideation system v1.0

Multifunctional Bio-inspired Structures and Devices

MBID stands for Multifunctional Bio-inspired Design, is a biological morphology-informed, rule-based concept design framework developed to translate functional biological structural strategies into novel multifunctional bio-inspired morphology-driven device and structural architectures.

APPLICATION

MBID v1.0 operates at the early stages of innovation, supporting concept generation, embodiment development, and initial functional verification. Concepts demonstrating technical feasibility and strategic relevance can then progress to detailed product development and commercialisation. The framework enables the structured combination of non-obvious or intentionally selected functions, resulting in distinctive multifunctional devices and structural concepts.

DESIGN SPACE AND APPLICATION SCALE

The MBID v1.0 design space is characterised by unstructured operating environments, the need to integrate multiple, often conflicting functions, constraint-heavy design contexts, and micro- to mesoscale solution spaces where scale-dependent physical laws govern feature-level function generation.

MBID v1.0 is particularly suited to problems where functionality emerges primarily from geometric and material feature interactions across scales rather than from assemblies of discrete components. It is therefore most directly applicable to design problems satisfying the above characteristics, regardless of industrial sectors. Examples include medical and surgical devices, bio-inspired robotic systems, and technologies for resource-constrained environments.

FOUNDATION

The MBID ideation system v1.0 guides the generation of morphology-driven multifunctional bio-inspired device concepts by systematically integrating biological morphological strategies into engineered geometry–material configurations to achieve coupled functional performance.

The framework facilitates systematic early-stage concept generation by integrating geometry and material across multiple length scales within a single structure, enabling functional coupling to emerge from form and material instead of relying on discrete components or add-on subsystems.

The effectiveness of the MBID ideation system is evidenced through cross-sector application and functional validation, including medical and surgical device - minimally invasive devices (painless mesoscale suture pins), passive robotic mobility device (non-pneumatic tyres), aerospace thermal-management device (heat-absorbing, low-pressure-drop architectures), and multifunctional wearable devices (marine-adaptive skins and impact-protection helmets).

DESIGN METHODOLOGY

The core of the system is a methodical framework comprising Domain Integrated Design (DID) Method and Expandable Domain Integrated Design (xDID) Model, which guide users in the systematic exploration and integration of functional biological morphologies.

The DID and xDID methodologies is the classification of biological morphological features based on their defining characteristics and their mapping to overarching geometric/architectural designations, referred to as Domains. These Domains are defined as Surfaces, Cellular Structures, Shapes, and Cross-Sections.

BIKAS - MORPHOLOGICAL DOMAIN INTEGRATION KNOWLEDGE BASE

BIKAS stands for Biologically Inspired Knowledge Acquisition and Simulacrum is the morphological domain integration knowledge base of the MBID framework. It comprises biological morphological features that are abstracted, classified, and mapped to their respective geometric/architectural designations.

BIKAS functions as both a morphological compass and a morphological-domain integration map, connecting morphological features, structural architectures, embodiment functions, and biological tissues. It is designed to reveal structure-embodied functional relationships within biological systems and guide concept generation.

BIKAS should not be interpreted as a biological repository.

Morphological features are organised under four geometric Domains: Surfaces, Cellular Structures, Shapes, and Cross-sections.

BIKAS is continuously evolving and it currently comprises 57 classified morphological features. The feature set expands, and the domain mappings are systematically refined as the methodology evolves.

The web application presents BIKAS through tree dendrogram visualisations. These dendrograms represent the hierarchical organisation of morphological features using a logic analogous to agglomerative clustering. Users navigate the Domains to explore, analyse, and integrate morphology-informed design strategies.
