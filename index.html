MBID ideation system v1.0

Multifunctional Bio-inspired Structures and Devices

MBID stands for Multifunctional Bio-inspired Design - a biological morphology-informed, rule-based concept design framework developed to translate functional biological structural strategies into novel multifunctional bio-inspired morphology-driven device and structural architectures.

APPLICATION

MBID v1.0 operates at the early stages of innovation, supporting concept definition, embodiment development, and functional verification. Concepts demonstrating technical feasibility and strategic potential advance to detailed product development and commercialisation. The framework enables the structured combination of non-obvious or intentionally selected functions, resulting in distinctive multifunctional device and structural concepts.

MBID v1.0 is applied at the early stages of design for critical and highly constrained industrial sectors, including medical and surgical devices, aerospace and defence systems, automotive and marine technologies, wearable systems, bio-inspired robotics, energy systems, and affordable technologies for resource-constrained environments.

APPLICATION SCALE:
MBID addresses morphology-driven design across scales, from architectural systems to macro-scale devices and miniature systems. The framework is particularly suited to morphology-driven miniature deivces and structures (micro- to centimetre scale), where functionality is governed by geometry and material properties rather than assemblies of discrete components.

FOUNDATION

The MBID ideation system v1.0 guides the generation of morphology-driven multifunctional bio-inspired device concepts by systematically integrating biological morphological strategies into engineered geometry–material configurations to achieve coupled functional performance.

TThe framework facilitates systematic early-stage concept generation by integrating geometry and material across multiple length scales within a single structure, enabling functional coupling to emerge from form and material instead of relying on discrete components or add-on subsystems.

The effectiveness of the MBID ideation system is evidenced through cross-sector application and functional validation, including medical and surgical device (painless meso-scale sutures), passive robotic mobility device (non-pneumatic tyres), aerospace thermal-management device (heat-absorbing, low-pressure-drop architectures), and multifunctional wearable devices (marine-adaptive skins and impact-protection helmets).

DESIGN METHODOLOGY

The core of the system is a methodical framework comprising Domain Integrated Design (DID) Method and Expandable Domain Integrated Design (xDID) Model, which guide users in the systematic exploration and integration of functional biological morphologies.

The DID and xDID methodologies is the classification of biological morphological features based on their defining characteristics and their mapping to overarching geometric/architectural designations, referred to as Domains. These Domains are defined as Surfaces, Cellular Structures, Shapes, and Cross-Sections.

BIKAS - MORPHOLOGICAL DOMAIN INTEGRATION KNOWLEDGE BASE

BIKAS stands for Biologically Inspired Knowledge Acquisition and Simulacrum is the morphological domain integration knowledge base of the MBID framework. It comprises biological morphological features that are abstracted, classified, and mapped to their respective geometric/architectural designations.

BIKAS functions as both a morphological compass and a morphological-domain integration map, connecting morphological features, structural architectures, embodiment functions, and biological tissues. It is designed to reveal structure-embodied functional relationships within biological systems and guide concept generation.

BIKAS should not be interpreted as a biological repository or compared with databases such as Ask Nature.

Morphological features are organised under four geometric Domains: Surfaces, Cellular Structures, Shapes, and Cross-sections.

BIKAS is continuously evolving and it currently comprises 57 classified morphological features. The feature set expands, and the domain mappings are systematically refined as the methodology evolves.

The web application presents BIKAS through tree dendrogram visualisations. These dendrograms represent the hierarchical organisation of morphological features using a logic analogous to agglomerative clustering. Users navigate the Domains to explore, analyse, and integrate morphology-informed design strategies.
