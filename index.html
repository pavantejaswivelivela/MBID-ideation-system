MBID stands for Multifunctional Bio-inspired Design - a biological morphology-informed, rule-based concept design framework developed to translate functional biological structural strategies into novel multifunctional mechanical architectures. The MBID applies to any field that benefits from multifunctional, morphology-driven structures, including aerospace & defense, medical and surgical devices, bio-inspired robotics (soft & micro), and energy and environmental systems.

Figure 1 illustrates the diverse applications of MBID across various sectors, including medical devices and instruments (e.g., painless sutures), passive multifunctional structures for robotics (e.g., non-pneumatic tires), aerospace components (e.g., heat-absorbing structures), marine systems (e.g., bio-inspired skins), and sports and performance wearables (e.g., helmets for protection)

DESIGN METHODOLOGY

The MBID ideation system provides a structured process bridging early-stage problem definition and embodiment design with functional verification. The core of the system is a methodical framework comprising the Domain Integrated Design (DID) and Expandable Domain Integrated Design (xDID) methodologies, which guide users in exploring and integrating functional morphologies. Figure 2 outlines the key components of the framework: identify, extract & abstract, classify & map, develop-visualise-analyse, select, and combine

BIKAS KNOWLEDGE BASE

BIKAS stands for Biologically Inspired Knowledge Acquisition and Simulacrum - serves as the core knowledge base for the MBID framework. BIKAS is an evolving repository of over 60 biological (morphological) features that have been abstracted, mapped, and classified based on their structural characteristics. This web application features a tree dendrogram visualization of the BIKAS knowledgebase, enabling users to explore and select biological features for multifunctional design applications.

Explore the dendrograms by clicking on geometric categories (DOMAINS) to reveal related biological features and their connections

The denrograms represent a hierarchical clustering of morphological features, using an approach analogous to agglomerative clustering. Features are grouped based on shared geometric characteristics/designation, allowing users to intuitively navigate and select combinations for multifunctional design applications.
