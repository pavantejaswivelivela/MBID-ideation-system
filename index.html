ABOUT

MBID (Multifunctional Bio-inspired Design) is a biological morphology-informed, rule-based product design and engineering framework for systematically generating multifunctional bio-inspired physically intelligent product concepts that can be translated into technically feasible products.

MBID enables the systematic integration of intentionally selected and unconventional functional combinations to create multifunctional bio-inspired physically intelligent products

MBID systematically translates biological physical intelligence into multifunctional bio-inspired physically intelligent engineering concepts for product design and engineering

Release Note

This release marks the first public release of MBID v1. Future versions will introduce refinements, expanded capabilities, AI-assisted workflows, and additional engineering demonstrations as the framework continues to evolve.

The latest updates, documentation, and future releases of the MBID framework are available at: MBID-GitHub

OBJECTIVE

The objective of MBID is to systematically translate biological morphology into multifunctional physically intelligent product concepts that can be realised as technically feasible products through engineering design, engineering verification, and ultimately experimental validation.

MBID leverages bio-inspired physical intelligence to design multifunctional systems that are resource- and energy-efficient, require little or no active electronic control, and exhibit robust, sustainable, safe, self-adaptive, and inherently easy-to-control behaviour.

APPLICATION

MBID supports concept generation, product architecture development, and system-level design, where functionality emerges from the interaction of geometry, material organisation, and structural hierarchy across multiple scales. The framework enables designers and engineers to systematically explore and integrate surfaces, cellular structures, shapes, and cross-sectional features to develop multifunctional solutions.

The versatility of MBID has been demonstrated through applications across multiple sectors, including minimally invasive medical and surgical devices (painless mesoscale suture pins), passive robotic mobility devices (non-pneumatic tyres), thermal-management devices (heat-absorbing, low-pressure-drop mesoscale architectures), and multifunctional wearable devices (marine-adaptive skins and impact-protection helmets).

FRAMEWORK AND DESIGN METHODOLOGY

The foundation of MBID is a systematic design methodology comprising the Domain Integrated Design (DID) Method and the Expandable Domain Integrated Design (xDID) Model. Together, these provide a structured process for exploring, selecting, and integrating biological morphologies into multifunctional engineering concepts.

CORE PRINCIPLES

Principle A

Function is embodied within biological morphology, material organisation, and structural hierarchy.

Principle B

Multifunctionality emerges through the systematic integration of embodied functions across domains and scales.

CORE RULES

Rule 1: Abstraction (Morphology Abstraction)

Abstract embodiment functions (functions embodied within biological morphology) from biological morphologies by identifying the relationship between integrated structures and their material organisation.

Rule 2: Classification & Mapping (Morphology Translation)

Classify and map biological morphologies into four engineering-relevant domains—Surfaces, Cellular Structures, Shapes, and Cross-sections—to facilitate their translation into engineering-relevant geometries.

Rule 3: BIKAS Exploration and Selection

Explore biologically inspired knowledge across unrelated organisms by identifying shared morphological strategies and functionally analogous solutions enabled by convergent evolution.

Rule 4: Systematic Integration (Morphology Combination)

Combine multiple morphological features using meta-level design parameters and multi-scale integration strategies to generate morphology-encoded multifunctional product concepts and engineering solutions.

FRAMEWORK

The DID and xDID methodologies is the classification of biological morphological features based on their defining characteristics and their mapping to overarching geometric/architectural designations, referred to as Domains. These Domains are defined as Surfaces, Cellular Structures, Shapes, and Cross-Sections.

BIKAS - MORPHOLOGICAL DOMAIN INTEGRATION KNOWLEDGE BASE

BIKAS stands for Biologically Inspired Knowledge Acquisition and Simulacrum is the morphological domain integration knowledge base of the MBID framework. It comprises biological morphological features that are abstracted, classified, and mapped to their respective geometric/architectural designations.

BIKAS functions as both a morphological compass and a morphological-domain integration map, connecting morphological features, structural architectures, embodiment functions, and biological tissues. It is designed to reveal structure-embodied functional relationships within biological systems and guide concept generation.

BIKAS should not be interpreted as a biological repository.

Morphological features are organised under four geometric Domains: Surfaces, Cellular Structures, Shapes, and Cross-sections.

BIKAS is continuously evolving and it currently comprises 57 classified morphological features. The feature set expands, and the domain mappings are systematically refined as the methodology evolves.

The web application presents BIKAS through tree dendrogram visualisations. These dendrograms represent the hierarchical organisation of morphological features using a logic analogous to agglomerative clustering. Users navigate the Domains to explore, analyse, and integrate morphology-informed design strategies.

SCOPE AND FUTURE DEVELOPMENT

MBID provides a structured interdisciplinary platform for generating multifunctional bio-inspired physically intelligent product concepts by bridging biological and engineering knowledge during early-stage design. This release represents MBID v1, with additional refinements and expanded capabilities to be explored in subsequent versions.
