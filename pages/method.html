DOMAIN INTEGRATED DESIGN (DID) / EXPANDABLE DID (xDID)

The core of the DID / xDID methodology is a structured classification, mapping and combination of functional morphological features from biological organisms. Unlike the other methodologies, DID / xDID focus on the systematic exploration of morphological features across diverse biological domains. This approach enables the generation of novel multifunctional design concepts by integrating features based on their geometric characteristics and functional capabilities.

MORPHOLOGY-DRIVEN APPROACH

Biological organisms achieve a wide range of functions through the interplay of morphology (structure), physiology (mechanisms), and behaviour. The DID / xDID approach explicitly focuses on functions that arise from morphological features, referred to as embodiment functions.

DIVERSITY-ENHANCED

Nature often provides efficient solutions. Distantly related organisms, despite distinct evolutionary paths, frequently converge on similar structural designs to perform the same functions. The DID / xDID uses this principle to explore morphological diversity across both plant and animal kingdoms, substantially expanding the design solution space for engineering challenges.

DID / xDID WORKFLOW

1. Identify functional morphological features from biological organisms

2. Extract the morphological feature and the embodiment function it performs. Abstract the embodiment function as a combination of the integrated structure and its structural strategy

3. Classify morphological features based on their defining characteristics and map them to overarching architectural and geometric designations, referred to as Domains. The Domains are Surfaces, Cellular Structures, Shapes, and Cross-Sections

For example, the morphological feature of micro-scale projections on the lotus leaf exhibits the embodiment function of water repellence. This function arises from the integrated structure formed by hierarchical micro- and nano-scale projections on the leaf surface and the associated structural strategy governing their arrangement and spacing. The material composition, particularly the presence of a hydrophobic wax layer, further contributes to this functionality. Within the DID / xDID framework, this feature is characterised as a body or skin texture and is mapped to the overarching architectural and geometric Domain of Surfaces

4. Develop a morphological knowledge base and visualize relationships between morphological features, embodiment functions, integrated structures, and structural strategies

5.Explore and select embodiment functions and corresponding morphological features within Domains. Meta-level design parameters are used to select between features that exhibit the same function and belong to the same Domain.

6. Combine selected features across Domains to design multifunctional bio-inspired structures

KEY DEFINITIONS

Morphological features: Morphological and anatomical features observed in the plant and animal kingdoms

Morphological features characteristic: The observable appearance, form, and physical attributes of a feature.

Embodiment function: The function exhibited by a physical structure as realised through its morphology

Integrated structure: The physical description of a multiscale, heterogeneous structure, including micro- and nanostructures, macro-scale geometry, and surface layers such as wax coatings.

Structural strategy: Encompasses aspects such as the arrangement, packing, or orientation of micro/nanostructures, symmetry, asymmetry, or tessellation patterns. It also accounts for changes in structural configuration due to external stimuli, such as the erection of scales or a change in skin compliance, which occur when other elements interact with the structure.

Domain: The overarching architectural or geometric designation used to organise morphological features.

Meta-level design parameters: Quantitative geometric parameters used to select between morphological features that belong to the same Domain and exhibit the same function. These parameters are validated through simulation-based case studies.

The parameters by domain are: Surfaces: Interaction area; Cellular Structures: Interaction area, porosity; Shapes: Scale; Cross-Sections: Scale.

In addition there are cross-domain selection parameters used when features from different Domains exhibit the same function. The parameters are Surface area-to-volume ratio; Stiffness-to-weight ratio.
